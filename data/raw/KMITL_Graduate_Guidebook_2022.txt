Regulations of King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang
Re: Graduate Education, B.E. 2565 (A.D. 2022)

Whereas it is deemed expedient to revise the regulations regarding graduate education of King
Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang to better suit the current graduate program management;

By virtue of Section 22(2) of the King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang Act, B.E.
2551 (A.D. 2008), in conjunction with the resolution of the Academic Council at its 8/2022 Meeting on
23 August 2022 and the Extraordinary Meeting on 26 August 2022, the resolution of the Institute
Council’s Academic Affairs Subcommittee at its 9/2022 Meeting on 2 September 2022, and the
resolution of King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang Council at its 10/2022 Meeting on 18
October 2022, these regulations are enacted as follows:

Article 1. These regulations shall be called “the Regulations of King Mongkut's Institute of
Technology Ladkrabang Re: Graduate Education, B.E. 2565 (A.D. 2022).”

Article 2. These regulations shall come into force from 18 October 2022 onwards.

Article 3. Regulations of King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang Re: Graduate
Education, B.E. 2564 (A.D. 2021), shall be repealed.

All provisions of other regulations, rules, orders, notifications, or resolutions insofar as they
deal with matters governed by these regulations or are contrary to or inconsistent with these regulations
shall be repealed and replaced by these regulations.

Article 4. The President shall hold the acting position hereunder, have the power to issue the
rules, notifications or orders of the Institute that are not contrary to these regulations, with approval of
the Academic Council, and report the same to the Institute Council.

In case of doubt, or if no provisions are specifically stated in these regulations, or if it is
necessary to specially provide a relief of requirements hereunder, judgment of the Academic Council
shall be final.

Rules set forth by the Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation shall be
applied in any instance where no other provisions are specifically stated in these regulations.

General Provisions

Article 5. In these regulations:
“Institute” means King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang.
“President” means the President of King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang.

“Student” means any student studying for any degree beyond that of the bachelors’ in King
Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang, including lecture-based trial student or research-based

“Academic division” means the academic division that offers educational programs beyond the

“Academic Division Committee” means a committee of an academic division in King

“Special semester” means summer session.

“Cooperation agreement” means the execution of a formal agreement between the Institute and
an outside organization to develop and manage a program with approval of the Institute Council and
such outside organization.

“Outside organization” means a higher education institution in or outside the country that has
been endorsed by an agency responsible for education of that country, or a governmental agency of the
department or equivalent level, or a state enterprise, or a public organization, or a private company
listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.

In case of a private company not listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand, it shall be at the sole
discretion of the Institute Council, provided that such private company is required to show its capability
and readiness to jointly produce quality graduates of such company that meet the education quality
standards.

“Independent study” means an independent study, individual study, or any professional paper
called by any other name that Plan 2 master’s students have to complete as partial fulfillment of degree
requirements.“Creative work” means academic work of any other form equivalent to a research paper, which
may include a work of art, artistic invention or innovative design that is original or newly created as a
product of proper and systematic research of a specific type of arts based on experiment or development
from an existing creative concept as a model or the ability to initiate a science that creates esthetic and
benefits recognized by a relevant professional community. According to ASEAN classification of arts,
the creative works include:

(1) Visual art, which consists of painting, sculpture, print, photograph, motion picture,
multimedia, architecture, and other design work;

other forms; and

“Thesis” means a systematic research study based on international academic research
principles. It is a course that students have to fulfil as partial fulfillment of degree requirements for Plan
1 master’s degree or doctoral degree.

“Qualifying examination” means an examination of basic knowledge, analytic skills,
capabilities and preparedness of doctoral students to independently conduct research. It is a
requirements for the doctoral students to pass this examination in order to be eligible to take the thesis
examination.

“Comprehensive examination” means the overall examination of the major and related fields of
study, whether it be a written or oral examination, to measure Plan 2 master’s students’ competency and
knowledge.

Chapter 2
Academic Administration

Article 6. The academic system is as follows:

6.1 The Institute operates a bi-semester academic system. The academic year is divided into
two regular semesters: the first semester and the second semester, and there may be a special semester.
One regular semester consists of not less than 15 weeks on instruction and the special semester may be
arranged as required by each academic division and with the number of class periods and credits
equaling those of a regular semester.

“ree6.2 The Institute may also operate other educational systems, such as tri-semester system,
module (group of courses) system, distance education, and others, provided that the period of study and
the number of credits are in proportion to those of the bi-semester system.

6.3 Each program offered by the Institute consists of a number of courses. Each course
organizes the content based on the number of credits. The number of credits granted for each course is
determined as follows:

6.3.1 A theory-based course entailing at least 15 hours of lectures or discussion or
instruction per one regular semester shall be measured as one credit in the bi-semester academic system.

6.3.2 A practice-based course entailing at least 30 hours of training or laboratory
sessions per one regular semester shall be measured as one credit in the bi-semester academic system.

6.3.3 The number of credits of a course involving both theoretical and practical
sessions will be measured in accordance with the criteria stipulated under 6.3.1 and 6.3.2.

6.3.4 Apprenticeship, fieldwork practice or other training entailing at least 45 hours per
one regular semester shall be measured as one credit in the bi-semester academic system. However, it
may be established that credit is not counted for such course.

6.3.5 Thesis, independent study, project work or any other activity assigned entailing
at least 45 hours per one regular semester shall be measured as one credit in the bi-semester academic
system.

6.3.6 With respect to education in the programs beyond the bachelor’s degree offered
by the Institute, the amount of contents of each course shall depend on the relevant program.

6.4 Every program offered must be approved by the Academic Council with consent of the
Institute Council before accepting students.

6.5 The Institute may offer programs that enable the graduates to receive two degrees or
implement other projects in accordance with rules or notifications of the Institute.

Article 7. Program structure

7.1 The Graduate Diploma Program and the Advanced Graduate Diploma Program, each of
which consists of courses carrying a minimum of 24 credits in total throughout the entire duration of the
program;

7.2. The Master’s Degree Program, which consists of a minimum of 36 credits in total
throughout the entire duration of the course and is composed of two plans as follows:

7.2.1 Plan 1 — Academic Program: this is a research-based program where students
have to submit a thesis and it is divided into two formats:7.2.1.1 Plan 1.1: this consists of a minimum of 36 credits of thesis. Students
may be required to take additional courses or carry out other academic activities without credits being
counted, but satisfactory achievement must be obtained.

7.2.1.2 Plan 1.2: this consists of a minimum of 12 credits of thesis and a
minimum of 12 credits of coursework.

7.2.2 Plan 2 — Professional Program: this is a coursework-based program where no
thesis is required; but there must be at least 3 credits and no more than 6 credits of an independent
study.

7.3. The Doctoral Program, which is available in two plans:

7.3.1 Plan 1 is a research-oriented program leading to a thesis that is expected to
contribute to the advancement of knowledge. Students may be required to take additional courses or
carry out other academic activities without credits being counted, but satisfactory achievement must be
obtained. Credits required for this type of plane are as follows:

7.3.1.1 Plan 1.1: candidates who have a master’s degree shall produce a thesis
worth not less than 48 credits;

7.3.1.2. Plan 2.1: candidates who have a bachelor’s degree shall produce a
thesis worth not less than 72 credits.

The thesis under 7.3.1.1 and 7.3.1.2 must have the same quality and standards.

7.3.2 Plan 2 is a research-oriented program that requires the students to complete a
thesis that is highly academically qualified and contributes to academic and professional advancement
of knowledge. There should also be additional courses. Credits required for this plan are as follows:

7.3.2.1 Plan 2.1: candidates who have a master’s degree shall produce a thesis
worth not less than 36 credits and take courses of at least 12 credits in total; and

7.3.2.2 Plan 2.2: candidates who have a bachelor’s degree shall produce a
thesis worth not less than 48 credits and take courses of at least 24 credits in total.

The thesis under 7.3.2.1 and 7.3.2.2 must have the same quality and standards.

Chapter 3
Academic Staff

Article 11. Categories, number, qualifications and characteristics of the academic staff:

11.1 “Full-time lecturer” means a person holding the position of lecturer, assistant
professor, associate professor, professor, or other equivalent position in the Institute, or a person in an
outside organization that enters into a cooperation agreement with the Institute, being responsible for
fulfilling the Institute’s missions, understanding the qualifications for the higher education, and working
full-time.

11.2 “Program lecturer” means a full-time lecturer whose qualification is corresponding or
related to the field of study of the program offered and is responsible for teaching or conducting
research in such field of study. One lecturer can be a program lecturer of many programs at the same
time, but his/her qualification must be corresponding or related to the field of study of those programs
and prior approval of the committee of the academic division, which is his/her organic unit, must be
obtained.

“Qualification related to the field of study of the program” means the qualification
established in the field of study standard. If there is no notification on the field of study standard or the
existing field of study standard has no provision relating to this matter, it shall refer to the qualification
related to the academic or professional matter of the program or any other qualification with direct
experience in the program obviously promoting the instruction in such program and enabling the
students to complete the objectives of the program. Consideration of the related qualification shall be at
the sole discretion of the Institute Council.

11.3 “Lecturer responsible for the program” means a program lecturer who is responsible
for managing and developing the program and instruction, from planning to quality control, monitoring
and assessment, and program development. The lecturer responsible for the program shall be involved
throughout the period of implementation of the program. A lecturer responsible for the program may
not be responsible for more than one program at a time. However, for multidisciplinary or
interdisciplinary programs, the lecturer responsible for the program can be responsible for another
program and there shall be no more than two repeated lecturers responsible for the program.

The same set of lecturers responsible for the program may be used for master’s degree and
doctoral degree programs of the same field of study.

11.8 Lecturers for the Graduate Diploma Program

11.8.1 A program lecturer shall hold at least a master’s degree or its equivalent and
have a minimum of three academic outputs, which are not a part of his/her study for a degree, published
in accordance with the criteria for appointing a person to hold an academic title during the past five
years, provided that, at a minimum, one output must be a research paper or creative work that has been
published to the general public.For a vocation-oriented program, the program lecturer shall be qualified in accordance
with the standards of such profession.

11.8.2 There shall be a minimum of three lecturers responsible for the program who
have a doctoral degree or the equivalent thereof, or a minimum of a master’s degree or the equivalent
thereof, and have produced in the past 5 years at least three academic papers which are not a part of
graduation requirements and published in accordance with the publication criteria specified for
academic title appointment, provided that, at a minimum, one output must be a research paper or
creative work that has been published to the general public.

In the case where a cooperation agreement is executed with an outside organization,
there shall be at least two program lecturers in the Institute being the lecturers responsible for the
program.

In case of extreme necessity for a field of study that suffers a lack of lecturers
responsible for the program or that has less than 10 students, the relevant academic division shall
present to the Institute the number and qualifications of the available lecturers responsible for the
program for consideration of the Higher Education Board on a case-by-case basis.

11.8.3 A lecturer giving instructions shall be a program or part-time lecturer who has
at least a master’s degree or the equivalent thereof in the particular field of study or a related field of
study or in the field of study of the course taught, has teaching experience, and has produced in the past
5 years at least one academic paper which is not a part of graduation requirements and published in
accordance with the publication criteria specified for academic title appointment.

A part-time lecturer may be exempt from a requirement for a master’s degree, but
he/she shall have a minimum of a bachelor’s degree or its equivalent, and has had work experience in
the course taught for not less than six years. The part-time lecturer’s teaching hours shall not exceed
50% of the course. If it is necessary for a part-time lecturer to teach over 50% of the total teaching
hours of a course, approval of the committee of the Academic Division owning the course and the
Academic Council shall be granted. Moreover, there shall be a full-time lecturer being responsible for
such course.

For a vocation-oriented program, a lecturer giving instructions shall be qualified in
accordance with the standards of such profession.

11.8.4 In case of a new lecturer having a doctoral degree, even though he/she does
not have any academic paper after graduation, he/she shall be permitted to serve as a teaching lecturer
of the graduate diploma program. However, if he/she wishes to serve as a program lecturer, he/she shall
have a minimum of one academic paper published within two years, two academic papers within four
years, or three academic papers within five years after graduation.

Leave of Absence, Resignation and Termination

Article 50. A leave of absence is a period of time when a student is not enrolled in classes in a
particular semester, provided that such leave of absence period will not be computed as part of the
duration of study in the program. The student will be granted the non-student status. A student may
apply for a leave of absence by giving a reason for such leave, which must be approved by the academic
division and recorded by Office of the Registrar. The student shall apply for a leave of absence for each
semester or every year. In the case where the student does not resume his/her study and does not apply
for an additional leave of absence, provided that he/she has lost contact for a total period of more than
three academic years, it shall be considered that his/her student status is terminated.

A student who wishes to maintain his/her student status during the leave of absence shall pay
the leave of absence fee for every regular semester. In case of the student’s failure to pay the leave of
absence fee, the Institute will transform the student’s status to a general person and keep his/her study
results in the credit bank. However, a general person who wishes to resume his/her study in the program
may pay the outstanding leave of absence fee to resume his/her student status and the study results,
courses, or modules stored in the credit bank will be transferred and can be used normally in the
program.

50.1 With regard to a leave of absence in a particular semester, if the student has enrolled
in courses, such enrollment shall be deemed to have been cancelled. All of the courses enrolled will not
be displayed on the transcript of record for the semester in which the leave of absence takes place. The
student will not be able to convert the “I” result to another grade.

50.2 The student, or his/her custodian in the case where the student is unable to be present
on his/her own, may apply for a leave of absence together with evidence, as the case may be, to
Director of the Office of the Registrar. Application for leave of absence must be completed before the
final examinations of the semester that the student wishes to take a leave of absence.

50.3 A student whose application for leave of absence is approved must make payment of
the leave of absence fee when he/she returns to study. Payment shall be made in accordance with the
Institute’s Notification.

50.4 A student who wishes to take a leave of absence for more than one academic year
must obtain approval of the Director of the Office of the Registrar.

Student’s Discipline

Article 54. Students must strictly conform to the discipline hereunder at all times. Failure to
comply with the following shall be regarded as violation of discipline and punishment shall be imposed
as defined in these regulations.

54.1 Students must dress appropriately.
54.2 Students must show respect to lecturers and staff of the Institute.

54.3 Students must behave in a polite and appropriate manner and must not constitute any
disgrace to themselves or the Institute.

54.4 Students must not drink liquors or consume intoxicants in the Institute.

54.5 Students must not smoke during instructions or examinations or in any areas in the
Institute where smoking is prohibited.

54.6 The following acts are considered serious disciplinary offenses:

54.6.1 Slander that causes others to suffer damages as well as instigating, supporting
or leading any chaotic event within the Institute’s premises, such as quarrel, destruction of property of
the Institute or governmental agency, delinquent acts, and demonstration consisting of more than 10
persons in violation of laws;

54.6.2 Drinking liquors or consuming intoxicants in the Institute;
54.6.3 Using illegal or narcotic drugs;

54.6.4 Carrying weapons or illegal articles;

54.6.5 Cheating in exams;

54.6.6 Displaying any behavior that is disrespectful to lecturers or staff members of
the Institute who carry out duties in accordance with laws or regulations or rules of the Institute, which
is deemed a serious disciplinary offense by the Academic Division Committee;

54.6.7 Forging another person’s signature and using it as evidence when contacting
the Institute, which causes damages to the Institute;

54.6.8 Engaging in any types of gambling in the Institute;54.6.9 Carrying out any act that damages or defames the Institute, such as being
hired to take exam for other persons, either in or outside the Institute;

54.6.10 Copying other person’s thesis or academic paper or independent study report
for oneself or any other person, or using the same academic paper with another person in applying for
graduation;

54.6.11 Hiring other person to write a thesis or independent study report for oneself
or any other person;

54.6.12 Carrying out any other act that is deemed a serious disciplinary offense by
the Discipline Committee. After being presented with the matter, the President is of the opinion that it is
serious.

Article 55. There are three modest disciplinary actions as follows:
55.1 Verbal warning;
55.2 Probation;
55.3 Compensation for damage caused.

Article 56. There are three serious disciplinary actions as follows:
56.1 Suspension of study;
56.2 Expulsion;
56.3. Dismissal.

Article 57. If the student commits any disciplinary offenses under Article 54, except for 54.6.5,
the President may order a punishment proportional to the offense committed. However, if there is any
reason for punishment mitigation, such reason may be taken into consideration to reduce the
punishment. After the President has issued and signed the punishment order, the academic division
shall, without delay, notify the student and the Office of the Registrar of such punishment order.

Article 58. In the case where the student cheats in exam under 54.6.5 and there is clear evidence

student’s offense as soon as possible from the date of detection and propose the investigation result to

punishment order, the academic division shall notify the student of such punishment order without
delay as well as the Office of the Registrar.

Article 59. In the case where the student commits any offense under Article 54, except for
cheating in exam under 54.6.5, the Discipline Committee appointed by the Institute shall have the
power to initiate a disciplinary action against the accused student immediately to investigate the offense
in a fair and quick manner. Recommended punishment shall then be submitted to the President for
approval. After the President has issued and signed a punishment order, the discipline committee shall
notify the student of such punishment order without delay as well as the chief of academic division that
the student belongs to and the Office of the Registrar.

Chapter 14
Dignity and Honor of Students to Receive the Degree

Article 61. Students who have honor and dignity to the extent that they are suitable for being
nominated by the Institute Council to receive a degree shall not only have culture, morality, maintain
good reputation, prestige and benefits of the Institute, comply with the student’s rules and regulations of
the Institute, but also need to have the following behaviors:

61.1 Not being diagnosed as a person of unsound mind by a medical doctor or adjudged as
incompetent or quasi-incompetent by the court;

61.2 Never having been imprisoned pursuant to a judgment or undergoing criminal
prosecution, except for offenses arising out of negligence or for petty offenses;

61.3 Not being a person of evil or immoral behavior, disgracing the Institute;

61.4 Not causing disunity or fight among fellow students or between the Institute’s
students and those of another institution or other persons;

61.5 Not displaying any behavior disrespectful or insulting towards the Institute’s lectures
or personnel;

61.6 Not interfering with the power to administer the Institute;

61.7 Not intentionally causing serious damage to the Institute’s property;

61.9 Not having any financial obligations due to the Institute.

Article 62. To apply for approval to attend the commencement ceremony, students shall comply
with and adhere to all requirements and procedures as well as date, time and place indicated in the
Institute’s educational calendar, and pay the fee to receive the degree as required by the Institute.

63.1 The student will not be nominated to receive the Institute’s degree;

63.2 Nomination for receiving the degree will be delayed for one to three academic years
